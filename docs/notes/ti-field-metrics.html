TI-Toolbox · field dosimetry note

Field metrics for temporal interference stimulation:
what we compute, and why

The temporal-interference literature reports a dozen named field metrics. Most of them are the same quantity written differently. This note separates the genuinely distinct axes from the restatements, sets out what the toolbox computes and what it deliberately omits, and records three claims checked against the implementation.

Reviewed against 196 papers in the project library, 34 read in full. All numeric results recomputed through the shipped code.

1What the toolbox computes

Five quantities are written for every simulation, two-pair and multipolar alike. A sixth is available but not written by default.

Note. Safety metrics are never opt-in. A user who forgets a checkbox does not learn they exceeded an exposure limit, and the cost is under a tenth of a second. What gets computed is not a user choice; the choice is what to look at, which is why field selection lives in the analyzer and not the simulator.

2Two numbers generate the envelope family

Project the per-pair fields onto a candidate direction \(\mathbf{n}\), and write \(a_k\), \(b_k\) for the signed projections of pair \(k\). Two quantities are sufficient:

\[ P = \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_i \left( \mathbf{E}_i \cdot \mathbf{n} \right)^2 \quad \text{(carrier power)} \] \[ Q = \left\lvert \sum_k a_k b_k \, e^{i\psi_k} \right\rvert \quad \text{(coherent pair coupling)} \]

Every envelope quantity is a function of that pair:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{modulation depth} &= \sqrt{2} \left( \sqrt{P+Q} - \sqrt{P-Q} \right) \\[2pt] \text{envelope peak} &= \sqrt{2(P+Q)} \\[2pt] \text{RMS} &= \sqrt{P} \end{aligned} \]

Three consequences decided most of the API. RMS is not a new metric — \(\mathrm{RMS}^2 \equiv P \equiv \mathrm{hf\_sar}/2\), exactly; a vector RMS is meaningless besides, since heating is isotropic. The envelope trough is free — \(\mathrm{env\_min} = \mathrm{env\_max} - \mathrm{depth}\) to \(4 \times 10^{-16}\). The modulation index adds little — \((\max - \min)/(\max + \min)\) correlates 0.88 with depth and is derivable after the fact.

At a single pair the depth formula collapses to \(2\min(\lvert a \rvert, \lvert b \rvert)\) — the Grossman closed form — to \(5 \times 10^{-13}\). The general form is not a rival theory; it is the same one, written so it survives past two carriers.

3Most published metrics are one quantity

This is the central result of the review. Six papers present what read as six different modulation-depth metrics. They are the same function, written for different conveniences.

Hirata 2024 [2]\(2\min\{\lVert \mathbf{E}_1 \rVert, \lVert \mathbf{E}_2 \rVert\}\) or \(2\lVert \mathbf{E}_1 \times \mathbf{E}_2 \rVert / \min\{\lVert \mathbf{E}_1 - \mathbf{E}_2 \rVert, \lVert \mathbf{E}_1 + \mathbf{E}_2 \rVert\}\)symmetric — removes the relabelling, keeps the two cases

Corazza settles this explicitly, proving the closed form is the solution to the max–min problem the other papers solve numerically, and noting the closed form is “preferred… to avoid time-consuming and potentially error-prone iterative solvers.” So a reader encountering five metric names is encountering one metric and four conveniences.

4The dose question is less open than it looks

TI's founding premise is that the kHz carrier is inert and the demodulated envelope is the dose. The experimental literature does not support that, and the disagreement is not marginal.

Table 4. Direct tests of envelope versus carrier.
StudyPreparationResult
Budde 2023 [10]rat sciatic nerve, in vivothresholds track total peak-to-peak; envelope contribution bounded below 6%
Semenov 2025 [11]isolated neurons, optical recordingmodulated thresholds never lower, often higher; no rectification asymmetry measured
Opançar 2025 [8]locust and human nervestrength–frequency curves statistically indistinguishable
Esmaeilpour 2021 [12]rat hippocampal sliceat 2 kHz, modulated and unmodulated thresholds are both 80 V/m
Wang 2023 [13]realistic L5 pyramidal modelTI thresholds indistinguishable from unmodulated high-frequency

The modelling literature agrees on the mechanism that undercuts the premise: passive membrane filtering cannot demodulate, because two carriers multiply rather than sum, leaving no spectral power at the beat frequency to filter toward [9, 14]. Any demodulation requires active rectification — and the same rectification produces off-target tonic firing and conduction block.

Consequence. hf_peak, which we file under safety, is close to the quantity Budde found actually predicts firing. If that holds, the functional/safety split in our registry is a regulatory convenience rather than a biophysical boundary, and hf_peak deserves to be read as a candidate dose, not only as a limit.

Two qualifications keep this honest. The strongest carrier-dominance evidence is suprathreshold; TI in humans is presumed subthreshold, where the mechanism is uncharacterized. And selectivity may live at the network level rather than the membrane [12]. The question is not closed — but the burden of proof has moved.

5Maximizing over direction can mislead

TI_max reports the best case over all orientations. Two findings say that number can be reached in a direction no neuron occupies.

  • Meng 2025 [5] computed the maximizing direction per voxel and found it in the left hippocampus is perpendicular to the long axis (84.5° ± 8.8°) — the orientation requiring the highest activation threshold.
  • Missey 2021 [15] measured the cost directly: montages with field lines parallel to Schaffer collaterals reached threshold at half the charge; perpendicular montages needed double. A fourfold swing, at identical envelope amplitude and identical focus location.

Huang 2023 [16] makes the same criticism of optimizers: they “only consider the spatial focality of the MD in a predefined direction, while ignoring the specificity of the modulation in different directions.” This is the argument for TI_avg. A maximum says nothing about how sharply peaked it is; the mean over directions does, and it costs nothing extra because the sweep is already running.

6Past two pairs, the architecture matters more than the formula

The review turned up three ways of handling more than two electrode pairs. Only two appear in the literature.

Table 5. Multipolar architectures.
MethodUsed by
Aevery pair assigned to \(f_1\) or \(f_2\); sum same-frequency fields linearly, then take the envelope onceLee 2022 [17], up to 10 pairs; Savvateev 2025 tripole [18]
Bindependent dyads, carriers separated by ≥1 kHzAcerbo 2023 / Botzanowski [4, 19]
Crecursive envelope-of-envelopesnobody

Architecture A is exact, because superposition happens before the nonlinear envelope step. That also explains why C fails: it applies the nonlinearity in the wrong order. We had measured C as wrong for N>2 — signed mean +38.6%, range −90% to +416% at four fields — and the literature independently never adopts it.

Open gap. Our API takes a list of fields and pairs them positionally, which assumes architecture B. Carrier-frequency assignment is never an input, so a Lee-style montage — where the correct computation is get_TI_vectors\(\left( \mathbf{E}_1 + \mathbf{E}_3, \; \mathbf{E}_2 + \mathbf{E}_4 \right)\) — would be computed as if it were four independent channels. This is a correctness hazard, not a documentation gap, and it is the same missing input needed to express a carrier-only field.

7Current ratio: separating envelope from exposure

Because the envelope is set by the weaker field, \(2\min(\lVert \mathbf{E}_1 \rVert, \lVert \mathbf{E}_2 \rVert)\), raising only the stronger carrier leaves it untouched while raising exposure. That is exact, not approximate.

Caveat. These ratios are injected currents. Holding \(\min(\lVert \mathbf{E}_1 \rVert, \lVert \mathbf{E}_2 \rVert)\) fixed at the target means solving for the currents that do so through the lead field — 5:8 at the scalp does not give 5:8 in the thalamus. The toolbox can compute this per subject, and should, before conditions are fixed.

8Where the two-carrier metrics agree, and where they part

It is often said that for two carriers the Grossman and Botzanowski metrics coincide. That holds only for collinear fields, and only for one of the two things called “the Botzanowski metric.”

At a well-targeted ROI the fields are near-collinear and the distinction vanishes. Off target they are not, and the two diverge — which is precisely the regime in which the metrics are supposed to be told apart. Both are implemented: get_mTI_vectors and get_magnitude_am.

9Adding a carrier: where our code takes a side

Add an unmodulated carrier to a modulated pair. The extended-Grossman view says the envelope is unchanged; the Botzanowski view says the extra energy raises the baseline and thins the modulation.

Adding carrier power at fixed coupling strictly reduces the envelope, since

\[ \frac{\partial (\text{depth})}{\partial P} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \left[ \frac{1}{\sqrt{P+Q}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{P-Q}} \right] < 0 \]

Because get_mTI_vectors is built on the (P,Q) form, the shipped TI_max already predicts thinning. That is a scientific position embedded in the implementation, not a neutral calculation, and it should be stated whenever multipolar focality numbers are reported. Measured: 18% at +2 mA, 38% at +5 mA, 52% at +8 mA, reproduced through the shipped function.

A separate category: ROI statistics

Much of what the literature calls a “metric” is a summary over a region, not a per-element field. These do not belong in the field registry and are listed here so the distinction stays clear: peak ratio and peak-sum ratio [17, 20], stimulated volume above a threshold [21], concentration and mis-stimulation ratios, the cube-root focal volume [16], the ratio to the worst off-target point, and the ROC-distance objective used by SimNIBS flex-search [22].

One is worth surfacing: the ratio to the worst off-target point. Our focality uses a gray-matter mean in the denominator, which is insensitive to a single off-target hotspot — a failure mode the literature identifies by name. Worth reporting alongside, and cheap.

On focality. Our own toolbox paper [23] reports that a 50% relative threshold raises focality 75% against a fixed threshold, while an 80% threshold lowers it 36.6%. The metric is that sensitive to an arbitrary choice. Any focality figure should carry its threshold, and a threshold-free measure should be reported beside it.

11Open items

  • Carrier-frequency assignment is not an input. Needed to distinguish architecture A from B, and to express a carrier-only field. The largest single gap.
  • Band separation is unchecked. Nothing verifies that carrier pairs are far enough apart for the envelope formulation to hold. Assumed silently, here and in the literature.
  • TI_normal for multipolar needs a fixed-direction envelope the API does not expose publicly.
  • The published description of TI_normal is looser than the code. The paper reads as projecting the maximized vector onto the normal; the implementation recomputes the envelope along the normal from the raw carrier fields, which is the correct and stricter operation.
  • No end-to-end validation. The formulation is verified against time-domain ground truth and brute force, but the mesh write path has not been exercised on a real subject.
