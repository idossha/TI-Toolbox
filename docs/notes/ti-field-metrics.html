What we compute, and why: TI/mTI field metrics

Every envelope metric in the toolbox reduces to two numbers per direction. This note sets out that formulation, states the decisions taken, and checks three claims from the R01 outline against the implementation.

01 The metrics we compute

Four quantities are written unconditionally for every simulation, 2-pair and multipolar alike. Two describe stimulation; two describe carrier exposure.

TI_normal — the envelope along the cortical surface normal — is also written, for 2-pair only. The N-pair analogue needs a fixed-direction envelope the API does not yet expose, and is deferred rather than approximated.

Design rule

Safety metrics are never opt-in. A user who forgets a checkbox does not learn they exceeded an exposure limit, and the cost is under a tenth of a second. What gets computed is not a user choice; the choice is what to look at, which is why the field selector lives in the analyzer and not the simulator.

02 Two numbers generate everything

Project the per-pair fields onto a candidate direction n. Write ak, bk for the signed projections of pair k. Then two quantities are sufficient:

P = ½ Σi (Ei·n)² carrier power Q = |Σk ak bk e^(iψk)| coherent pair coupling

Every envelope quantity is a function of that pair:

modulation depth = √2 ( √(P+Q) − √(P−Q) ) envelope peak = √(2(P+Q)) RMS = √P

Three consequences follow immediately, and they decided most of the API.

  • RMS is not a new metric. RMS² ≡ P ≡ hf_sar/2, exactly. It was already in the toolbox under another name. A vector RMS is meaningless besides — heating is isotropic.
  • The envelope trough is free. env_min = env_max − depth, to 4×10−16. Storing it would be storing a subtraction.
  • The modulation index adds little. (max−min)/(max+min) correlates 0.88 with depth across random fields and is derivable post hoc.

At a single pair the depth formula collapses to 2·min(|a|,|b|) — the Grossman 1: closed form — to 5×10−13. The general form is not a different theory; it is the same one, written so it survives past two carriers.

03 The dose question, in the time domain

The outline's premise is that nobody knows which feature of the waveform a neuron transduces. The (P,Q) form makes the candidate doses explicit rather than rhetorical: the envelope swing, the carrier peak, the carrier power, and the mixing term Q itself are four different functions on the same two numbers. They can be moved independently, and that is what the current-ratio design exploits.

Because the envelope is set by the weaker field, 2·min(|E1|,|E2|), raising only the stronger one leaves it untouched. That is the whole trick, and it is exact rather than approximate.

One caveat worth designing around

These ratios are injected currents; the table assumes they map linearly to field magnitudes at the target. They do not, in general — the field at an ROI depends on lead-field geometry, so holding min(|E1|,|E2|) fixed at the target means solving for the currents that do so, not assuming 5/8 at the scalp gives 5:8 in the thalamus. The toolbox can compute this per subject, and it should, before the conditions are fixed.

05 Where the two-carrier metrics actually agree

The outline states that for two carriers the Grossman and Botzanowski metrics are mathematically identical. That needs one qualification, and it happens to matter for Aim 2.

Needs qualifying. Both statements can be true because “the Botzanowski metric” names two different things. The directional form — project, then take the envelope, then maximize over direction — is our TI_max and is identical to Grossman at K = 1. The running-RMS form in the appendix takes the envelope of ‖E(t)‖ with no direction search, and differs by up to a factor of two.

This is not pedantry. At a well-targeted ROI the two fields are near-collinear and the distinction vanishes. Off target they are not, and the metrics diverge — which is precisely the regime Aim 2 proposes to measure. The divergence is a feature of the experiment, but the two forms have to be named separately for the prediction to be stated at all. Both are implemented: get_mTI_vectors and get_magnitude_am.

06 Adding a carrier: where our code takes a side

Aim 2's cleanest manipulation is a modulated pair plus an unmodulated carrier. The extended-Grossman view says the envelope is unchanged. The Botzanowski view says the extra energy raises the baseline and thins the modulation.

Confirmed, and it is already a commitment in the code. Adding carrier power at fixed coupling strictly reduces the envelope, because

∂(depth)/∂P = (√2/2) [ 1/√(P+Q) − 1/√(P−Q) ] < 0

Since get_mTI_vectors is built on the (P,Q) form, the shipped TI_max already predicts thinning. That is a scientific position embedded in the implementation, not a neutral calculation, and it should be stated plainly whenever mTI focality numbers are reported. Measured thinning: 18% at +2 mA, 38% at +5 mA, 52% at +8 mA.

Gap this exposes

The API cannot yet express “unipolar plus extra HF” directly. Fields are paired positionally, so a carrier with no beating partner has nowhere to go — it should contribute to P (and to both safety metrics) but not to Q. Supporting carrier-only fields is a small change and a prerequisite for simulating the Aim 2 conditions.

The one metric adopted outright from the collaborator branch is the orientation average, shipped as TI_avg. It costs nothing — it falls out of the direction sweep already being run — and it answers a question TI_max structurally cannot, since a maximum over direction tells you nothing about how sharply peaked that maximum is.

08 Open gaps

  • Carrier-only fields. Needed for the Aim 2 conditions; small change.
  • Band separation is unchecked. Nothing anywhere verifies that carrier pairs are far enough apart in frequency for the envelope formulation to hold. It is assumed silently, on both sides of the collaboration.
  • TI_normal for mTI. Requires the fixed-direction envelope.
  • End-to-end validation. The formulation and the numbers here are verified against time-domain ground truth and brute force, but the mesh write path has not been exercised against a real subject.
