The Blender extension was built to enable publication level visualization and presentation of simulation results using other 3D modeling software in general, and specifically Blender. It provides a convenient interface for exporting cortical surfaces, field vectors, electrode placements, skin surfaces, and sub-cortical structures in formats compatible with Blender, CAD software, and other 3D visualization tools. Use it when you want a guided workflow from the desktop application, or call the underlying Python scripts directly for batch jobs and automation.

Enable **3D visual exporter** in **Settings ▸ Extensions** to add it to the navigation rail.

## Preview and export

Choose the subject, simulation, mode (**Cortical regions**, **Field vectors**, **Montage
visualizer**, **Sub-cortical** or **Networks**) and its settings on the left, then press **Run export**. The
right pane shows the plan, an interactive Scene tab and the live Terminal, which follows the
export's job log. A cortical-regions export queues two jobs, one STL and one PLY; both logs remain
available after they finish. To cancel an export, select its job in **Jobs** and choose **Stop**.
Exports are written under `derivatives/ti-toolbox/visual_exports/sub-<id>/`.

- **Cortical regions:** preview the selected subject's atlas and click regions to update the
  same selection used by the export. The whole grey matter surface is always exported; with an
  empty selection it is the only output.
- **Sub-cortical:** preview the selected segmentation volume; clicking a labelled region toggles
  its export selection. Selected labels are outlined in the full atlas. This is the source
  segmentation, not a preview of final mesh cleanup.
- **Montage:** display the subject's surfaces and the EEG net recorded with the selected
  simulation. The montage-only setting hides unused net positions. Markers show electrode
  positions; configured electrode dimensions are applied when the export is generated.
- **Field vectors:** inspect the simulation's field context. Arrow sampling and geometry are
  generated by the export itself.
- **Networks:** the **Renders** tab shows the figures and videos already rendered for the simulation,
  by network, and picks up new ones as they finish.

Missing inputs produce a preview message; the preview does not substitute another subject.

## Supported Modes

### Networks

This mode renders white-matter networks of a simulation as one PNG figure per colouring, an optional MP4
turntable and an optional Blender scene, under `visual_exports/sub-<id>/<sim>/networks/<datetime>/`. It needs the subject's tractogram and network; without them the
tab says what to run (**Pre-processing ▸ Diffusion ▸ Tractography**).

**Choosing a network.** Four ways, all ending in the same list of region pairs, which the 3D pane
draws as you go, with a streamline count beside it. The name follows the choice until you type your
own.

- **Preset:** a set of atlas regions on the subject's own labels: frontal, posterior and temporal
  thalamocortical, hippocampal-cortical, cingulate-cortical, precentral-brainstem, insular-cortical,
  amygdala-cortical, basal ganglia-cortical and interhemispheric cortical. A preset is a set of
  regions, not an anatomical claim, and what it holds depends on the tractography. One whose labels
  this network does not have, or with no streamline in it, is shown disabled with the reason.
- **By region:** **From** regions and, optionally, **To** regions. With no To, every edge the From
  regions have.
- **By connection:** seed regions, then edges from their table or by clicking lines in the 3D pane.
- **Strongest here:** the top N connections of this simulation in a metric you name. None is
  preselected and none is recommended. It reads the simulation's edge table, which **Assess
  connections** writes if the simulation has none yet.
- **Your own file** loads a tractogram you made for this head model, as TRX, TCK or TRK, in the head
  model's world coordinates in millimetres. See [below](#your-own-network-file).

The same choosing appears in the Optimizer's Network target, apart from Strongest here and Your own file,
so a mask and a render can come from one definition.

**Same-side thalamocortical networks.** Thalamocortical networks keep same-hemisphere connections by
default, following common tractography practice, because crossed streamlines are often artefactual;
this is a specificity choice, since modest crossed projections exist in the macaque, and the removed
count is reported and can be turned off. Same-side is decided by the subject's own atlas labels
(`Left-Thalamus-Proper` with `lh.*`, `Right-Thalamus-Proper` with `rh.*`), not by a midline plane. The
picker's count line and the Plan state what was left out, for example
`1,407 of 1,598; 191 opposite-hemisphere removed (right thalamus to left cortex 167, left thalamus to
right cortex 24)`. `network.json` records the same counts and how many kept streamlines reach more than
5 mm past the midline and back, which labels cannot catch.

Same-side filtering may remove real connections for medial, orbital and limbic frontal targets:
**Include opposite-hemisphere connections** is the way to check. It adds the crossed edges and adds
`_with_opposite` to the network's name, so both versions sit side by side. Only thalamocortical presets
are filtered: **By region**, **By connection**, **Strongest here** and every other preset draw what they
select.

References: Behrens et al. 2003, *Nat Neurosci*, [doi:10.1038/nn1075](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1075);
Akram et al. 2018, *NeuroImage Clin*,
[doi:10.1016/j.nicl.2018.01.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nicl.2018.01.008); Warrington et al. 2020,
*NeuroImage*, [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.116923](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.116923);
Dermon & Barbas 1994, *J Comp Neurol*,
[doi:10.1002/cne.903440403](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.903440403); Preuss & Goldman-Rakic 1987,
*J Comp Neurol*, [doi:10.1002/cne.902570211](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.902570211).

**Running.** **Run export** on a network the simulation cannot draw yet queues the tract job's `assess`
stage first (about 3 min under emulation; fields along every streamline, one small `<name>.trx`), and the
render waits for it. A simulation with `all.trx` needs no assess.

**The figure.** One image per colouring, holding only the model and its tractography: no title, caption,
region names, colour bar or panel labels. Four panels on one scale and one black background, two to a row:

| | left | right |
|---|---|---|
| top | **lateral**, the side the network is on, left for a bilateral one | **superior**, anterior at the top |
| bottom | **anterior**, facing the head | **posterior** |

**Where renders go.** One folder per run:

```
visual_exports/sub-<id>/<sim>/networks/<datetime>/
  ti_max.png  direction.png
  ti_max_turntable.mp4  direction_turntable.mp4
  network.blend
  network.json
```

**`network.json`** is the one sidecar. `metrics` holds what the picture does not carry: the streamlines in
the network, the opposite-hemisphere counts left out, the streamlines per region, the median `TI_max`,
`ti_along` and `align_index` over the network (per streamline, the arc-length mean, as the assess stage
reports), and the montage, by electrode label or by coordinates to 0.1 mm. `metrics.images` holds per file
the streamlines drawn and how they were sampled, the shell's level and how it was chosen, the colour map and
range (`colour_scale`) or the direction key (`colour_key`), and the panel order. None of these is ranked over
another. Beside `metrics` it holds the selection, the code version and, per colour, the automatic setup used,
the files, and the config to render it again: `simnibs_python -m tit.blender network.json ti_max`.

Each run has a folder of its own, named by date and time as an ex-search run is (`20261106_143022`), so a render
never replaces an earlier one and there is nothing to name. Tick **TI_max** and **Fibre direction** together and one
run writes both colours to one folder, with one `network.json`; a colour added in a later run gets its own folder. The Plan names what it writes. The render's working bundle is deleted when
the render succeeds, and kept when it fails (`bundle_<colour>/`), or always with `keep_bundle` in the config.
The **Renders** tab beside **Terminal** and **Scene** shows them by run, labelled with what the network is (its
preset, its regions or your file's name) and the date and time, one open at a time; it also reads folders rendered
before this form, under their own names.

**The Scene pane** fills the column while you choose. The Plan is one line above it, opening on a click, and
**Layers** at the pane's corner opens the legend and the opacity sliders over the canvas, with a **Cortex** switch, on by default, that shows or hides the cortex so the fibres inside it can be seen. Regions are clicked on the cortex, so seeds are chosen with it on, or in the form. The switch changes this pane only, not the render.

**Setup.** The **Setup** section of the form lists what the render will use, with a reason for each when you
rest on it, and replaces any one value.

| Setting | Automatic rule |
|---|---|
| Lit regions | The regions it was chosen by, else its two busiest ends, and at most 24 parcels |
| Lateral panel | Left when each hemisphere holds at least a quarter of its ends; else the hemisphere holding more of them |
| Streamlines drawn | All up to 8,000, then an even sample, and `network.json` says how many |
| Tube radius | 0.09 mm from about 1,600 streamlines up, wider below it, up to four times |
| Colour window | 99.5th percentile along the drawn fibres |
| TI_max shell | Brain p95, off only below 10 streamlines, which a shell would hide |

Other options:

- **Colour by:** **Fibre direction (RGB)**, the local tangent, or **TI_max**, the field magnitude
  along each fibre averaged over ±3 mm for display. A turntable in TI_max opens in fibre direction and
  crossfades.
- **Figure**, and a **Turntable video**, which is off until you ask for it and holds no text either.
- **Export .blend**, off until you ask for it: `network.blend` in the run's folder, the scene the
  render used, with its meshes and materials in the file so it opens on another machine. It has a camera
  per panel. It holds one colour, TI_max when both are ticked, and the figure's scene when a figure and a
  turntable are both asked for; `network.json` says
  which it holds. The Plan states its approximate size, and `network.json` its size in bytes.
- **Quality:** **Fast preview** (a 1600x1600 figure, a 4 s turntable at 960x540) checks a choice before
  **Final** (a 4000x4000 figure, a 5 s turntable at 1920x1080).
Every colour shows a field descriptor along the fibres, not a prediction of activation.

Renders run in the container's Blender. On Apple Silicon that is emulated, so a preview frame takes about
a minute. A bundle kept with `keep_bundle` in the config renders in seconds in a local Blender.

#### Your own network file

Pick the file and the toolbox stores it under the subject, at `tractography/own/`. It is checked before
anything is queued, and refused with the reason if it has no streamlines, has points that are not
numbers, or has fewer than 95 % of its points inside this head model's brain. Coordinates are never moved
or converted: a file in another space (for example QSIPrep's ACPC) is refused, not fixed.

The checks show the streamlines fall inside this head model's brain. They cannot prove the file was made for
this subject: another subject's file in a similar space, or one flipped left to right, can pass.

Each streamline's two ends get a region from the head model's atlas, and the form says how many have an end
in no region. Those are still drawn. **Run** assesses the file on the simulation first if needed, then
renders every streamline with the same automatic setup as any network, to
`networks/<datetime>/`. `network.json` records the source filename, the checks and the unlabelled count under
`metrics.source`. A file cannot be a target in the Optimizer.

**What is produced:**

- **STL files**: Binary STL format containing only geometry (no color information). These are lightweight files suitable for CAD software, 3D printing, or any workflow that doesn't require color mapping.
- **PLY files**: PLY format with embedded color information. Each region is color-mapped according to the field values (e.g., `TI_max`) from the simulation mesh. These files preserve both geometry and field data for visualization in Blender or other 3D tools.

**Output structure** (under `derivatives/ti-toolbox/visual_exports/sub-<id>/<simulation>/`):

- STL: `stl/cortical_stls/regions/<region>.stl` and `stl/cortical_stls/whole_gm.stl`.
- PLY: `ply/cortical_plys/regions/<region>.ply` and `ply/cortical_plys/whole_gm.ply`.
- The app always keeps the intermediate `.msh` files of the PLY export for audit trails and debugging: `ply/meshes/<region>_region.msh` and `ply/whole_gm.msh`.

**What is produced:**

- **CH1, CH2** — the first two channels' electric fields in V/m (red, blue), optional.
- **TI** (`mTI` for four channels) — the TI_max vector: its length is TI_max over all channels and it points along the direction of maximum modulation (green).
- **TI_sum** — the vector sum of all channel fields (E1 + E2 for two channels) (yellow), optional.
- **TI_normal** — the TI envelope along the cortical normal, drawn outward (cyan), optional.

For what each field means, see [Field definitions]({{ site.baseurl }}/wiki/field-definitions/#quantities-of-interest).

**Vector properties:**

- Each arrow's direction represents the electric field direction at that point.
- Arrow length is scaled by the field magnitude (configurable via length scale parameter).
- Colors can be mapped using RGB mode (fixed colors per vector type: CH1=red, CH2=blue, TI=green, TI_sum=yellow, TI_normal=cyan) or magnitude-based scaling (magscale) to visualize field intensity across a blue-green-red gradient.
- Vector width and anchor point (tail or head) are customizable for visualization clarity.

**TI vs mTI modes:**

- **TI mode**: Uses 2 channel meshes (CH1 and CH2) to compute standard TI vectors.
- **mTI mode**: Uses 4 channel meshes (CH1, CH2, CH3, CH4) to compute multi-channel TI vectors, enabling more complex stimulation patterns.

**Sampling options:**

- Configure the number of vectors to sample (default 10,000 in the app, 100,000 in the Python API) or use all nodes for maximum detail.
- When using the maximum sample count, the script will produce a vector on every node of the given surface mesh. This is an expensive process that requires a large amount of memory and can take multiple hours for all vectors to be produced.
- Random seed controls reproducibility of sampling patterns.
- From the Python API, `top_percent` keeps only the strongest vectors by magnitude (for example `top_percent=10`).

**What is produced** (under `derivatives/ti-toolbox/visual_exports/sub-<id>/montage_publication/`):

- **`<id>_<simulation>_montage_publication.blend`**: the publication scene with scalp, grey matter, electrode objects and text labels, sensible cameras, lighting conditions and material transparency.

**Electrode configuration:**

### Sub-cortical Extraction

This mode extracts sub-cortical structures from NIfTI segmentation files and converts them to 3D mesh formats. It can extract specific anatomical regions by label or export the entire segmentation volume.

**What is produced** (under `derivatives/ti-toolbox/visual_exports/sub-<id>/sub-cortical/`):

- **STL files**: Binary STL format geometry files suitable for CAD software and 3D printing
- **MSH files**: SimNIBS mesh format for compatibility with simulation workflows
- **PLY files**: the same mesh coloured by a simulation field (`TI_max` by default), written when a simulation is selected; its subject-space field volume is written from the simulation's field mesh first if the simulation did not save it
- **NIfTI files**: Copied segmentation files for reference

**Segmentation options:**

- Extract specific anatomical labels (e.g., thalamus: 10,49) or export the entire volume
- Optional cleaning of small disconnected components to reduce mesh complexity
- Automatic label extraction from FreeSurfer or other segmentation atlases

**Input sources:**

- Default: `m2m_<subject>/segmentation/labeling.nii.gz` (the charm segmentation)
- Custom: Any NIfTI file containing labeled anatomical regions

## Python API

The blender module exposes typed dataclass configs and runner functions for programmatic use:

```python
from tit.blender import (
    RegionConfig,
    VectorConfig,
    MontageConfig,
    MontageResult,
    run_regions,
    run_vectors,
    run_montage,
)

# Example: export cortical regions as PLY
# Paths (mesh, m2m, output dir) are resolved from the project via PathManager
config = RegionConfig(
    subject_id="001",
    simulation_name="HIPP_L",
    format=RegionConfig.Format.PLY,
    atlas="DK40",
    field_name="TI_max",
)
run_regions(config)
```

## Tips and troubleshooting

- **Environment**: Run Python API scripts with `simnibs_python` inside the TI-Toolbox container. A saved JSON config runs with `simnibs_python -m tit.blender config.json`.
- **Large vector clouds**: Start with a smaller **Sample count** (`count` in the Python API), or set `top_percent` from the Python API, to reduce file sizes before ramping up density.
- **Label extraction**: When extracting specific labels, use comma-separated values (e.g., "10,49" for left/right thalamus). Check FreeSurfer's label lookup table for anatomical region codes.
- **Error logs**: The job's **Terminal** shows the export's output; see [Where are the logs]({{ site.baseurl }}/wiki/troubleshooting/#where-are-the-logs) for the saved log files.

## Blender Tutorial: Visualizing PLY Files

This tutorial walks you through importing and visualizing PLY files exported from TI-Toolbox in Blender, including how to display the embedded color attributes.

### Step 1: Importing PLY Files

1. Open Blender and start with the default scene (or create a new project).
2. Go to **File** → **Import** → **Stanford (.ply)**.
3. Navigate to your exported PLY file location: cortical regions are in `derivatives/ti-toolbox/visual_exports/sub-<id>/<simulation>/ply/cortical_plys/` (`whole_gm.ply` and `regions/`), field vectors in `<simulation>/vectors/`, and sub-cortical meshes in `sub-<id>/sub-cortical/`.
4. Select your PLY file and click **Import Stanford (.ply)**.

The mesh will appear in your viewport. If the mesh appears very small or very large, you may need to adjust the view or scale the object.

![Import PLY file]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/imgs/blender/visual_exporter_blender_1.png)

### Step 2: Switching to Material Preview

To see materials and colors properly, switch the viewport shading mode:

1. In the 3D viewport, locate the viewport shading buttons in the top-right corner (or press `Z` to open the viewport shading menu).
2. Select **Material Preview** mode (or press `Z` then `2`).

Material Preview mode provides a quick preview of how materials will look with basic lighting, making it ideal for checking color attributes.

![Material Preview mode]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/imgs/blender/visual_exporter_blender_2.png)

### Step 3: Adding a Material

To display the color attributes from your PLY file, you need to create and assign a material:

1. Select your imported PLY mesh object in the viewport.
2. In the **Material Properties** panel (right sidebar, indicated by a sphere icon), click **New** to create a new material.
3. The material will be automatically assigned to your selected object.

![Adding a material]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/imgs/blender/visual_exporter_blender_3.png)

### Step 4: Setting Color to the 'Col' Attribute

To use the color data embedded in your PLY file, set it as the material's base color. Blender 4.4 imports the PLY's vertex colors as a color attribute named **`Col`** (case-sensitive), which is also the mesh's active color attribute:

1. With your object still selected, in the **Material Properties** panel, locate the **Base Color** field (under the **Surface** section).
2. Click on the **Base Color** field.
3. Select **Color Attribute** from the dropdown menu.
4. In the attribute name field that appears, enter `Col` (capital C). Leaving the name blank also works, because it then uses the active color attribute.

Your mesh should now display the color-mapped field data from your simulation. The colors represent the field values (e.g., `TI_max`) that were exported with the mesh.
